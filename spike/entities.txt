ANSWER: To cook a Cuban Boston butt pork roast in the oven, you first need to prepare a seasoning mix. Puree garlic, oregano, olive oil, limes, and salt in a blender or food processor. Place the pork butt roast in a roasting pan, score the fat on top of the pork, and rub the garlic mixture all over it. Cover and refrigerate the meat overnight or up to 24 hours, making sure to flip the roast occasionally and redistribute the rub. Preheat the oven to 250 degrees and remove the meat from the refrigerator 30 minutes before cooking. Place the pan in the oven, fat side up, and add 1 cup of water to the pan. Roast the meat uncovered, occasionally basting it with juices and turning the pan for even cooking. Cook the roast for 6 to 8 hours, until the meat easily shreds. After removing the roast from the oven, let it rest for 20 minutes, then pull the roast into shreds with a fork, leaving the meat in the pan juices. It's now ready to serve.

QUESTION: how the colonists respond to the stamp act. why was it so upsetting?

ANSWER: The colonists responded to the Stamp Act of 1765 with a variety of actions, including both violent and non-violent protests. They held peaceful protests, some of which escalated into violence when British soldiers intervened. The colonists also organized public protests, legislative resolutions, town meetings, and boycotts of British goods. The Stamp Act was so upsetting to the colonists because it was the first direct tax imposed by Parliament and required the use of tax-stamped paper for all newspapers, legal documents, and other items. This was seen as an infringement on their rights as there was no colonial representation in Parliament. Thus, the slogan "No Taxation without Representation" became popular.

QUESTION: wat is dna

ANSWER: DNA, or Deoxyribonucleic acid, is a nucleic acid that contains the genetic instructions used in the development and functioning of all known living organisms and some viruses. It is made up of molecules called nucleotides, which include a phosphate group, a sugar group, and a nitrogen base. The four types of nitrogen bases are adenine, thymine, guanine, and cytosine. The order of these bases determines the genetic code. DNA is structured like a double helix, similar to a ladder with base pairs forming the rungs and the sugar and phosphate molecules forming the vertical sidepieces. An important attribute of DNA is its ability to replicate, or make copies of itself.
