DNA, or Deoxyribonucleic acid, is a nucleic acid that contains the genetic instructions used in the development and functioning of all known living organisms and some viruses. It is made up of molecules called nucleotides, which include a phosphate group, a sugar group, and a nitrogen base. The four types of nitrogen bases are adenine, thymine, guanine, and cytosine. The order of these bases determines the genetic code. DNA is structured like a double helix, similar to a ladder with base pairs forming the rungs and the sugar and phosphate molecules forming the vertical sidepieces.
