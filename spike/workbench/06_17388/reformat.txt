- The primary difference between a cold and sinusitis, or a sinus infection, is the duration of the illness.
- Colds generally peak after two to three days, whereas sinus infections can last seven days or more.
- People with a common cold generally do not run a fever or experience significant fatigue, unlike those with a sinus infection.
- Sinusitis involves the sinus cavities of the skull, while a cold only affects the mucous membranes.
- Sinusitis is usually due to bacterial agents, while a cold is due to viral agents.
- The headache in sinusitis is much heavier than in a cold, and tends to worsen when bending forward.
- The fever in sinusitis is greater, and the nasal discharge is more yellowish, while in a cold, the nasal blockage tends to be relieved in a couple of days, while in sinusitis, it can persist for some time.
