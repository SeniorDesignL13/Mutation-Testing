ORIGINAL ANSWER
The colonists responded to the Stamp Act of 1765 with a variety of actions, including both violent and non-violent protests. They held peaceful protests, some of which escalated into violence when British soldiers intervened. The colonists also organized public protests, legislative resolutions, town meetings, and boycotts of British goods. The Stamp Act was so upsetting to the colonists because it was the first direct tax imposed by Parliament and required the use of tax-stamped paper for all newspapers, legal documents, and other items. This was seen as an infringement on their rights as there was no colonial representation in Parliament. Thus, the slogan "No Taxation without Representation" became popular.
